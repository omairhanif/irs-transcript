# IRS Transcript Code 152 Explained: What It Means for Your Tax Refund

When checking your tax refund status, you might come across IRS Tax Topic 152 and wonder what it means for your refund. Seeing an unfamiliar IRS code can cause confusion, especially when you are waiting for money to arrive in your bank account. Many people worry that this message indicates an error, an audit, or a problem with their tax return.

When checking your refund status, Tax Topic 152 is a general message indicating that the IRS is processing your return. It does not mean your refund has been approved, rejected, or placed under review. It also does not guarantee a specific refund date.

Understanding IRS Tax Topic 152 can help you interpret your refund status, identify possible delays, and determine whether you need to take any action.

This guide explains what IRS Tax Topic 152 means, why it appears, how it affects your refund, how long processing might take, and what you should do if your refund is delayed.

## What Is IRS Transcript Code 152?

IRS Tax Topic 152, often called Code 152, is a reference taxpayers may see when checking their federal income tax refund status through the IRS [Where's My Refund?](https://www.irs.gov/refunds) tool.

It generally appears alongside information explaining that the IRS is processing your return and that additional time may be needed.

Seeing Tax Topic 152 does not automatically mean something is wrong with your return. It is not an audit notice, a rejection, or proof that the IRS has found an error.

For example, suppose you filed your tax return electronically and expected your refund within three weeks. When you check your refund status, you see a message referencing Tax Topic 152.

This means you should continue monitoring your refund status while the IRS processes your return. The message alone does not explain why processing may take longer or provide a specific payment date.

It is also important to distinguish between Tax Topic 152 and a three-digit transaction code appearing in the transaction history section of an IRS account transcript. These are not the same. Tax Topic 152 is a refund-related reference, while transaction codes identify activities recorded in IRS account records.

## What Does IRS Code 152 Mean for Your Tax Refund?

The appearance of Tax Topic 152 generally means your refund is still being handled.

However, it does not tell you when the IRS will issue your refund.

Your return may still be going through processing, or additional time may be needed for verification and other checks.

Here are some important things to understand:

- **Your return is being processed:** The IRS has not necessarily completed all processing steps.
- **Your refund might take longer:** Additional time may be needed before the IRS determines your refund amount.
- **It does not mean there is a mistake:** The reference alone does not indicate that your return contains incorrect information.
- **It does not mean you are being audited:** Tax Topic 152 is not an audit notification.
- **No immediate action is usually required:** Unless the IRS requests additional information or sends you a notice, you generally do not need to submit paperwork just because this topic appears.

The main point is that Tax Topic 152 is informational. Use the IRS refund tracker to monitor changes and follow any instructions the IRS provides.

## Why Does IRS Tax Topic 152 Show Up?

There are several possible reasons you might see Tax Topic 152 when checking your refund status.

The message does not point to one specific issue. Instead, it may appear while your return is going through the refund process or when additional processing time is required.

### 1. Your Tax Return Is Still Being Processed

One common reason is that the IRS has not finished processing your return.

After receiving a tax return, the IRS performs several steps. These may include checking the information provided, reviewing reported income, calculating taxes, and determining the refund amount.

Even if you file electronically, processing does not happen immediately.

If you recently submitted your return, seeing Tax Topic 152 might simply mean you need to wait for another update.

### 2. More Verification Is Needed

The IRS may need additional time to verify information on your tax return.

For example, the agency might need to compare your return with documents from employers, banks, or other organizations.

This does not automatically mean you made a mistake.

If the IRS identifies a discrepancy that requires further examination, processing could take longer.

### 3. You Claimed Certain Tax Credits

Some tax credits require additional verification before the IRS can issue a refund.

Two important examples are:

- Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC).
- Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC).

Under federal law, refunds involving these credits generally cannot be issued before mid-February, even if you file early.

This restriction helps the IRS verify claims and prevent fraudulent refund payments.

If your return includes either of these credits, you may have to wait longer than taxpayers who do not claim them.

### 4. You Filed an Amended Tax Return

Amended tax returns generally require more processing time than original returns.

If you submitted Form 1040-X to correct information on a previously filed return, the IRS must review both the original return and the updated information.

This additional work can take time.

Remember that the standard Where's My Refund? tool is designed for original tax returns. For amended returns, use the separate [Where's My Amended Return?](https://www.irs.gov/filing/wheres-my-amended-return) service.

### 5. You Submitted an Injured Spouse Claim

If you filed Form 8379, Injured Spouse Allocation, your refund may require additional processing time.

This form is used when a joint refund could be applied to certain debts belonging to one spouse, and the other spouse wants to protect their share.

The IRS must review the allocation before determining how much of the refund each spouse should receive.

This process can take longer than usual.

### 6. You Applied for an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number

Some taxpayers submit an application for an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) along with their tax return.

An ITIN application may require additional processing and verification.

As a result, the entire return and refund process could take longer.

### 7. Your Return Needs Manual Review

Some tax returns cannot be processed through the IRS's standard automated systems.

For example, missing information, unusual reporting details, or discrepancies may require additional attention.

When this happens, the IRS may need more time before finalizing the return.

Tax Topic 152 does not confirm that a manual review is taking place. You should monitor your refund status and wait for an IRS notice if additional information is required.

## Does IRS Code 152 Mean Your Refund Is Approved?

No. Tax Topic 152 does not confirm that your refund has been approved.

It is important to understand the difference between a processing message and an actual refund issuance.

When you see Tax Topic 152, the IRS may still be processing your return. Your refund amount and payment date may not have been finalized.

To determine whether a refund has actually been issued, look for **Transaction Code 846** on your IRS Tax Account Transcript.

Transaction Code 846 indicates that the IRS has issued a refund. It is different from Tax Topic 152.

Therefore, if you want to know whether your refund has actually been issued, do not rely on Tax Topic 152 alone.

## How Long Does It Take to Get a Refund After Tax Topic 152 Shows Up?

There is no specific number of days associated with Tax Topic 152.

The time it takes to receive your refund depends on how you filed, whether your return requires additional checks, and whether the IRS needs more information.

According to general IRS processing guidance, most refunds for electronically filed returns are issued within 21 days. Paper returns generally take six weeks or longer.

However, these are estimated time frames, not guaranteed deadlines.

Here is a general overview:

| Filing or refund situation | Processing time |
| --- | --- |
| Electronic filing | Usually within 21 days |
| Paper filing | Six weeks or more |
| EITC or ACTC refund | Generally cannot be issued before mid-February |
| Amended return | May take several months |
| Return requiring additional checks | Depends on the review |

If you still see Tax Topic 152 after the expected processing period, continue checking your refund status and look for any instructions from the IRS.

Do not submit another original tax return simply because processing is taking longer than expected.

## What Should You Do If You See IRS Code 152?

In most cases, you do not need to take immediate action when Tax Topic 152 appears.

However, you can follow a few steps to stay informed and make sure you do not miss important IRS updates.

### Step 1: Check Your Refund Status

Visit the official [IRS refund tracker](https://www.irs.gov/refunds).

Select **Where's My Refund?** and provide the required information.

You will generally need:

- Your Social Security Number (SSN) or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN).
- Your filing status.
- The exact refund amount shown on your tax return.
- The tax year.

Review the information to see whether the IRS has provided a new message or status update.

### Step 2: Look at Your IRS Online Account

Sign in to your IRS Individual Online Account to review your tax records and notices.

You may find additional information about your account, including transcript changes and other important updates.

Remember that an IRS account transcript and the Where's My Refund? tool serve different purposes. The refund tracker is generally the best place to check your current refund status.

### Step 3: Look for IRS Notices

The IRS may send a notice if it needs additional information, identifies a problem, or makes changes to your refund.

Check your mail and any available online notices.

If you receive a letter, read it carefully and follow the instructions provided.

Some notices require a response within a specific period. Failing to respond may cause additional delays.

### Step 4: Wait for the Next Update

The IRS generally updates Where's My Refund? once every 24 hours, usually overnight.

Checking repeatedly throughout the day is unlikely to provide additional information.

If your return is still being processed and the IRS has not requested anything, continue monitoring the refund tracker.

### Step 5: Contact the IRS When Needed

If your refund is taking longer than expected or the refund tracker instructs you to contact the IRS, use the official contact information.

Important IRS phone numbers include:

- **Refund hotline:** 800-829-1954.
- **Individual tax assistance:** 800-829-1040.

Keep a copy of your filed return and any related tax documents available when speaking with the IRS.

## IRS Code 152 vs. Common IRS Refund Codes

When reviewing your refund status or tax transcript, you might encounter several IRS codes.

Understanding their differences can help you avoid confusing a processing message with a refund approval or account adjustment.

### IRS Tax Topic 151

Tax Topic 151 is a message indicating that the IRS has changed or may change your refund.

It could mean that your refund is affected by a tax adjustment, deduction, or another issue requiring attention.

If you see Tax Topic 151, read the accompanying message carefully and wait for an official IRS notice explaining your situation.

Do not assume that every Tax Topic 151 situation is the same.

### IRS Transaction Code 150

Transaction Code 150 generally means that your original tax return has been posted to your IRS account and a tax liability has been assessed.

It is an important entry when reviewing your account transcript.

However, Code 150 does not mean your refund has been issued.

### IRS Transaction Code 570

Transaction Code 570 generally indicates that additional action is pending on your account.

It can appear when the IRS needs to complete processing or review before issuing a refund.

The code itself does not explain why the delay is happening.

### IRS Transaction Code 971

Transaction Code 971 indicates that an additional action or notice has been recorded on your IRS account.

Its meaning depends on the associated action code and your account history.

If Code 971 appears alongside other transactions, review the surrounding entries and any IRS notices before drawing conclusions.

### IRS Transaction Code 846

Transaction Code 846 is one of the most important codes to look for when tracking your refund.

It indicates that the IRS has issued a refund.

Your transcript usually shows the refund amount and the date it was issued.

If you see Code 846, check the amount and date to understand the refund being issued.

## What Does It Mean If IRS Code 152 Disappears?

If Tax Topic 152 disappears from your refund status, it does not automatically mean your refund has been approved or denied.

The IRS may have updated your return information, or the message may have changed as your return moved through the processing system.

The next status may provide additional details about your refund.

For example:

- Your refund might be moving toward issuance.
- The IRS might display a new processing message.
- Additional review might be required.
- The IRS might send a notice requesting more information.
- Your refund might eventually be issued.

Always review the latest message in Where's My Refund? rather than interpreting the disappearance of Tax Topic 152 on its own.

## Can IRS Code 152 Become Code 846?

Yes. A taxpayer who sees Tax Topic 152 during refund processing might eventually receive a refund.

However, Tax Topic 152 does not automatically change into Transaction Code 846.

These identifiers serve different purposes. Tax Topic 152 is a refund-related reference, while Code 846 records an actual refund issuance on your IRS account transcript.

Once your return has completed processing and the IRS determines that a refund is due, Transaction Code 846 may appear on your account transcript.

If you see Code 846, check the amount and date. The payment may take additional time to reach your bank account, depending on the payment method and financial institution.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Is IRS Code 152 Good or Bad?

Tax Topic 152 is generally neither good nor bad. It is an informational message related to refund processing. It does not mean your return contains an error, that you are being audited, or that your refund has been approved.

### 2. Does Tax Topic 152 Mean My Refund Is Delayed?

It can appear while your return is still being processed and additional time may be needed. However, the message alone does not confirm that there is a delay or explain how long processing will take.

### 3. Should I Contact the IRS After Seeing Code 152?

You generally do not need to contact the IRS just because Tax Topic 152 appears. Continue checking Where's My Refund? Contact the IRS if the tracker instructs you to do so or if you need assistance with a specific issue.

### 4. Does IRS Code 152 Mean I Am Being Audited?

No. Tax Topic 152 is not an indication of an audit. If the IRS selects your return for examination, it will generally send correspondence explaining the situation.

### 5. Can I Still Get My Refund With Tax Topic 152?

Yes. Tax Topic 152 does not mean your refund has been denied. Your return may still be going through processing, and the IRS may issue your refund after completing the necessary steps.

### 6. How Often Does the IRS Update Code 152?

The Where's My Refund? tool generally updates once every 24 hours, usually overnight. Checking it multiple times during the day is unlikely to provide additional information.

### 7. What Happens If Code 152 Changes to Code 151?

Tax Topic 151 indicates that an adjustment or another issue may affect your refund. Read the message from the IRS carefully and wait for an official notice explaining any changes or actions required.

### 8. Does Code 152 Have a Specific Refund Date?

No. Tax Topic 152 does not provide a specific refund date. Use the IRS refund tracker to monitor your refund status and look for a payment update.

### 9. Can I Find Code 152 on My IRS Transcript?

Tax Topic 152 is generally associated with the Where's My Refund? service rather than an entry on an IRS account transcript. Your transcript may contain transaction codes such as 150, 570, 971, and 846.

### 10. What Does Code 846 Mean After Tax Topic 152?

Transaction Code 846 indicates that the IRS has issued your refund. If it appears after you previously saw Tax Topic 152, it generally means the refund issuance process has been recorded on your account.

## Final Thoughts

IRS Tax Topic 152 is a message taxpayers may see when checking their federal income tax refund status.

Although seeing an unfamiliar code can be worrying, it does not automatically mean there is a problem with your return. It generally appears in connection with refund processing and indicates that additional time may be needed.

The best approach is to monitor your refund through the IRS Where's My Refund? tool, read any notices you receive, and follow the instructions provided by the IRS.

Remember that Tax Topic 152 does not guarantee a refund date or confirm that your refund has been approved. If you want to know whether the IRS has issued your refund, look for Transaction Code 846 on your IRS Tax Account Transcript.

Understanding the difference between these identifiers can help you interpret your refund status more accurately and avoid unnecessary worry while waiting for your tax refund.
