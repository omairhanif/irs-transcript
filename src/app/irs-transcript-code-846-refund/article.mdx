# IRS Transcript Code 846: What It Means for Your Tax Refund

Seeing **IRS Transcript Code 846** on your account transcript is usually good news. It generally means the IRS has issued your tax refund and recorded the payment on your account. After waiting for your tax return to be processed, this is often the update taxpayers hope to see.

However, there is a difference between the IRS issuing a refund and the money arriving in your bank account or mailbox. The date and amount shown beside Code 846 can help you understand what the IRS has sent, but they do not guarantee that your bank will make the funds available immediately.

In this guide, you will learn what IRS Code 846 means, how to find the refund date, how long it may take to receive your money, and what to do if your refund has not arrived.

## What Does IRS Transcript Code 846 Mean?

**IRS Transaction Code 846 means “Refund of Overpayment.”** It indicates that the IRS has recorded a refund payment for an overpayment on your tax account.

In simple terms, the IRS has processed the refund transaction and released the amount shown on your account transcript. Depending on your refund arrangements, the payment may be sent by direct deposit or as a paper check.

The IRS technical reference, Document 6209, describes Code 846 as a transaction that debits the tax module for the amount of overpaid tax, plus applicable interest, to be refunded to the taxpayer.

When you see Code 846, pay attention to three details:

- **Transaction code:** 846 identifies the refund transaction.
- **Date:** The date associated with the refund being issued.
- **Amount:** The refund amount recorded in that transaction.

For example, a transcript might show:

| Code | Description | Date | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| 846 | Refund issued | March 12, 2026 | $2,450.00 |

This fictional example indicates that the IRS recorded a refund of $2,450 with an issue date of March 12, 2026.

**Important:** Code 846 confirms that a refund transaction was issued. It does not necessarily mean the money is already available in your bank account.

## Is IRS Code 846 Good News?

Yes. In most situations, Code 846 is a positive sign because it means the IRS has recorded the issuance of your refund.

Before Code 846 appears, your tax return generally goes through processing, verification, and other required checks. Once the refund transaction is posted, the IRS has recorded a refund payment.

However, Code 846 does not guarantee that you will receive the amount you originally expected. Your refund may have been reduced because of changes to your return or certain outstanding debts. Compare the amount next to Code 846 with your expected refund and the other transactions on your transcript.

## Where Can You Find Code 846 on Your IRS Transcript?

You can find Code 846 on your **IRS Account Transcript**. This transcript provides a record of transactions posted to your tax account, including payments, credits, adjustments, and refunds.

To locate it:

1. Visit [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/).
2. Sign in to your IRS Individual Online Account.
3. Open the **Tax Records** section.
4. Select the option to view your tax transcripts.
5. Choose the Account Transcript for the tax year you want to review.
6. Open the transcript and find the transactions section.
7. Look for the entry labeled **Refund Issued** or transaction code **846**.

Review the date and amount displayed on that line. If you cannot find Code 846, the IRS may not have posted a refund transaction for that tax year yet. Continue checking your account and use the IRS refund tracker for the latest available status.

## How to Read the Date Next to IRS Code 846

The date beside Code 846 helps show when the IRS recorded the refund as issued. It is not necessarily the date your bank will display the deposit or the date a paper check will arrive.

For example:

| Code | Date | Amount |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 846 | April 8, 2026 | $1,850.00 |

In this fictional example, April 8 is the refund issue date recorded on the transcript. If you chose direct deposit, your bank may need additional time to receive and process the payment. If you chose a paper check, delivery depends on mailing and postal processing.

Do not assume that a refund is missing simply because it does not appear in your account on the date shown.

## How Long After Code 846 Will You Receive Your Refund?

The time between Code 846 and receiving your refund depends on the payment method and whether any issues occur after the IRS issues the payment.

### 1. Direct deposit

If you selected direct deposit, the refund is sent electronically to the bank account provided on your tax return. Your financial institution may need time to receive and process the payment. Check your bank account around the issue date and review pending transactions if available.

If the deposit does not appear, confirm that the bank account information on your return was correct.

### 2. Paper check

If you selected a paper check, the IRS sends the refund to the mailing address associated with your tax account. Delivery can take longer than an electronic payment. Check your mailbox regularly and make sure the address on file with the IRS is current.

If the check does not arrive, use IRS refund information tools to determine whether further action is necessary.

### 3. Bank processing delays

Even after the IRS issues a refund, your bank controls when funds become available. Possible reasons for a delay include:

- Bank processing schedules
- Weekends or federal holidays
- Incorrect account information
- A restricted bank account
- Additional review by the financial institution

The IRS cannot control every step of your bank's payment-processing procedure.

## Why Is My Refund Not in My Bank Account After Code 846?

It can be frustrating to see Code 846 on your transcript but not find the money in your bank account. There are several possible explanations.

### Incorrect bank account information

If the routing or account number on your tax return was incorrect, the financial institution may reject the deposit. The bank may return the payment to the IRS, which can then take steps to issue the refund, potentially as a paper check.

Check your tax return to confirm the banking information you provided.

### Bank processing time

Your bank may not make the funds available immediately after the IRS releases the payment. Allow time for processing, or contact your bank and ask whether it received an incoming federal tax refund payment.

### Refund sent as a paper check

Sometimes a taxpayer expects a deposit, but the IRS sends a paper check instead. For example, a financial institution may reject a deposit because the account is closed or does not meet the requirements for receiving the payment.

Check your IRS refund status for information about the payment method.

### Refund offset

Your refund may be reduced because the government applied some or all of it toward outstanding debts. Possible debts include:

- Federal income taxes
- Certain past-due child support obligations
- Eligible federal agency debts
- Certain state tax obligations

If an offset applies, you may receive less than you originally expected. Review your transcript and any IRS or Treasury notices for information.

## What If Code 846 Shows a Refund Amount Lower Than Expected?

If the amount beside Code 846 is lower than the refund you calculated on your tax return, do not assume the IRS made a mistake. The IRS may have adjusted your refund during processing or applied part of it to an outstanding debt.

Compare the transcript amount with your tax return, then review the transaction codes and amounts posted to your account. Some codes can indicate credits, tax adjustments, payments, or transfers.

| Code | General meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 150 | Tax return filed and tax assessed |
| 806 | Withholding credit |
| 766 | Credit to your account |
| 768 | Earned Income Tax Credit |
| 570 | Additional account action or processing hold |
| 846 | Refund issued |
| 898 | Refund applied to a debt through the Treasury Offset Program |

These codes give context. Review dates, amounts, and descriptions together; one code alone does not always explain a refund difference. If you think the IRS adjusted your refund incorrectly, read any notice you receive and follow its instructions.

## Can Code 846 Appear More Than Once?

Yes, you may see multiple Code 846 entries on an account transcript. This does not automatically mean that the IRS issued multiple separate refunds.

Multiple entries may be linked to refund transactions, tax account activity, or a refund reissued after an earlier payment was returned or canceled. Compare:

- The dates of each transaction
- The amounts shown
- Any related refund or transfer codes
- The tax period linked to the entries

Do not add the amounts together without understanding what each transaction represents.

## What Is the Difference Between IRS Code 846 and Code 841?

Codes 846 and 841 describe refund-related events:

| Code | Meaning | What it indicates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 846 | Refund issued | The IRS recorded a refund payment. |
| 841 | Refund canceled | A previously issued refund payment was canceled. |

If the IRS issues a refund but a direct deposit is rejected and returned, you may see related transactions on your transcript. Code 841 can indicate that a refund payment was canceled, and a later Code 846 may be associated with a replacement refund. Review the dates and amounts to understand the sequence.

## How to Check Your Refund After Code 846

The IRS [Where's My Refund?](https://www.irs.gov/wheres-my-refund) tool lets taxpayers check the status of an income tax refund. You usually need:

- Your Social Security number or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)
- Your filing status
- The whole-dollar amount of your expected refund

The tool shows three stages:

1. **Return Received:** The IRS received your tax return.
2. **Refund Approved:** The IRS approved your refund and provides a personalized refund date.
3. **Refund Sent:** The IRS sent your refund through the selected payment method.

The refund tracker and your account transcript provide different information. Use both when you need to understand your refund status.

According to the IRS, you can usually start checking within:

- 24 hours after electronically filing a current-year return
- Three or four days after electronically filing a prior-year return
- Four weeks after mailing a paper return

## What Should You Do If Your Refund Has Not Arrived?

If Code 846 appears but you have not received your refund, take these steps:

1. **Confirm the refund issue date:** Open your Account Transcript, find Code 846, and note the date and amount.
2. **Check Where's My Refund?:** Visit the IRS refund tracker for available payment information.
3. **Review your payment method:** Check whether you selected direct deposit or a paper check.
4. **Contact your bank:** If you chose direct deposit, ask whether the bank received or rejected the payment.
5. **Check your mailing address:** If you expected a paper check, make sure your address is correct and watch your mail.
6. **Contact the IRS if the payment is still missing:** Follow IRS instructions for a missing refund. The IRS may start a refund trace when applicable conditions are met.

For a paper check, the IRS generally allows taxpayers to start a claim for a replacement check when more than 28 days have passed since the refund was mailed, provided the refund tracker indicates that this option is available. Follow the current IRS instructions for your situation.

## Frequently Asked Questions About IRS Transcript Code 846

### Does IRS Code 846 mean my refund is approved?

Code 846 means the IRS recorded a refund issuance transaction. It is generally a sign that the refund has been released, although the money may not yet be available in your bank account or mailbox.

### Does Code 846 show the date I will receive my refund?

Not necessarily. The date beside Code 846 is the refund issue date recorded on your transcript. Your bank or postal service may need additional time to deliver the payment.

### How long does direct deposit take after Code 846?

There is no guaranteed number of hours or days for every taxpayer. Timing depends on when the payment reaches your financial institution and when the bank processes it.

### Can my refund still be delayed after Code 846?

Yes. Bank processing problems, incorrect account information, rejected deposits, and other payment-related issues can affect when you receive your money.

### Why does Code 846 show an amount different from my tax return?

The IRS may have adjusted your refund or applied part of it toward an eligible outstanding debt. Compare your transcript transactions and review any notices explaining the adjustment.

### What does an amount next to Code 846 mean?

IRS account transcripts use accounting conventions in which debits and credits may appear with signs. A refund transaction can therefore appear as a negative amount. Review the transaction description and account balance rather than interpreting the minus sign as a refund reduction.

### Can Code 846 appear for an amended tax return?

Yes. Refund-related transactions can appear after an amended return is processed. Code 846 alone does not confirm that every issue connected with an amended return has been resolved.

### What if Code 846 appears but I never receive my refund?

Check the IRS refund tracker, confirm your payment method, and contact your bank if you selected direct deposit. If you expected a paper check, check your mailing address. Follow IRS instructions for a missing refund or refund trace.

### Is Code 846 the same as Code 150?

No. Code 150 generally indicates that the original tax return transaction was posted and tax assessed. Code 846 indicates that a refund transaction was issued. They represent different account activities.

### Should I contact the IRS after seeing Code 846?

Usually, there is no need to contact the IRS simply because Code 846 appears. First, check the issue date and your refund status. Contact the IRS if the payment is missing and the applicable waiting period or refund-tracker instructions indicate that follow-up is necessary.

## Conclusion

IRS Transcript Code 846 is generally good news because it means the IRS has recorded your refund as issued. The date and amount beside the code help you understand when the refund transaction was processed and how much was released.

However, an issued refund is not always money already received. Direct deposits can require bank processing, paper checks can take time to arrive, and payment issues can delay delivery.

If you see Code 846, review the transaction date and amount, check your refund status through the IRS tool, and monitor your bank account or mailbox. If the payment does not arrive, follow IRS instructions for investigating a missing refund.
