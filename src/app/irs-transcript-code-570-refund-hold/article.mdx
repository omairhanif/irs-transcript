# IRS Transcript Code 570: Why Is Your Tax Refund on Hold?

Seeing **IRS Transcript Code 570** can be stressful, especially when you are expecting a tax refund and have been waiting for the IRS to process your return. This code generally indicates that additional action is pending on your tax account or that a credit hold has been placed on it.

In simple terms, the IRS may need more time to review or resolve something before releasing your refund. Code 570 does not automatically mean your tax return contains an error, that you are being audited, or that your refund has been permanently denied.

Understanding the transaction codes, dates, and amounts on your IRS Account Transcript can help you follow what may be happening and whether you need to take action.

## What Does IRS Transcript Code 570 Mean?

**IRS Transaction Code 570 generally means “Additional Liability Pending and/or Credit Hold.”**

According to IRS Document 6209, Code 570 can indicate that additional tax liability is pending or that a credit is being held. It can place a freeze on an account module, preventing the IRS from refunding or transferring the credit while the matter remains unresolved.

In plain language, this usually means the IRS has not finished processing an issue connected to your tax account.

For example, a transcript might show:

| Transaction | Date | Amount |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Tax return filed (150) | March 9, 2026 | $0.00 |
| Withholding credit (806) | April 15, 2025 | -$5,200.00 |
| Additional account action pending (570) | March 9, 2026 | $0.00 |

This is an illustrative example, not an actual IRS account. It shows how Code 570 may appear alongside other transactions.

**Code 570 does not explain the exact reason for the hold by itself.** Review surrounding transactions, dates, amounts, and any IRS notices for more context.

## Why Is IRS Code 570 on Your Tax Transcript?

Code 570 can appear in several situations. The specific reason depends on your account and the IRS processing activity.

### 1. The IRS needs to review your tax return

The IRS may need time to review information reported on your return before releasing a refund. Information may require verification or an account adjustment. Some returns need additional processing even when the taxpayer filed accurately.

### 2. Income information needs verification

The IRS receives income information from employers, banks, and other reporting organizations. If information on your return does not match records reported to the IRS, additional review may be necessary. Examples may include:

- W-2 wages that do not match employer records
- Missing or incorrectly reported 1099 income
- Differences in reported tax withholding
- Income information that has not yet been received or matched

These are possible circumstances, not guaranteed explanations for every Code 570 entry.

### 3. Tax credits require additional review

Some tax credits have eligibility requirements and may require verification. The IRS may need to review information related to the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC), Child Tax Credit (CTC), Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC), or education-related credits. If it needs to verify eligibility or reconcile information, your account may be delayed.

### 4. Additional tax liability may be pending

One description of Code 570 refers to additional liability pending. The IRS may be reviewing an issue that could affect the amount of tax you owe. If an adjustment is necessary, transactions may appear that change your refund or tax balance.

### 5. Identity or return verification

In some situations, the IRS may need to verify your identity or confirm that you filed the return. Code 570 alone does not confirm that an identity verification request has been issued. If the IRS needs you to verify your identity or provide information, it will generally provide instructions through a notice or online service.

### 6. Other account-related issues

Code 570 can also be associated with other account conditions, including credit holds or pending account actions. Not every Code 570 entry has the same cause.

## Does IRS Code 570 Mean Your Refund Is on Hold?

For individual income tax accounts, Code 570 generally indicates that an account action is pending and a credit may be held. If your account has a credit balance, the hold may prevent the IRS from issuing a refund until the relevant issue is resolved.

The exact effect depends on your account circumstances. Code 570 is not a complete explanation of your refund status.

To understand whether your refund is delayed:

1. Open your IRS Account Transcript.
2. Find the Code 570 entry.
3. Review transactions before and after it.
4. Check whether a refund transaction, such as Code 846, appears.
5. Look for any related notice or action code.
6. Check your refund status using the IRS [Where's My Refund?](https://www.irs.gov/wheres-my-refund) tool.

If Code 846 appears later, it generally indicates that the IRS recorded a refund issuance transaction.

## What Is the Difference Between IRS Code 570 and Code 571?

Codes 570 and 571 are related, but they indicate different account events.

| Code | General meaning | What it indicates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 570 | Additional liability pending and/or credit hold | A hold or pending account action is present. |
| 571 | Reversal of the freeze associated with Code 570 | The relevant freeze has been reversed. |

For example, a transcript might show:

| Transaction | Date | Amount |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Account action pending (570) | March 9, 2026 | $0.00 |
| Reversal of freeze (571) | April 6, 2026 | $0.00 |
| Refund issued (846) | April 6, 2026 | -$2,800.00 |

This illustrates one possible sequence in which a hold is followed by its release and then a refund issuance. Not every Code 570 entry is followed by Code 571; other transactions or account actions may occur.

## What Does Code 570 Followed by Code 971 Mean?

Code 971 is associated with miscellaneous account actions. Its meaning depends on the action code and other information attached to the transaction. In some situations, it is associated with an IRS notice being issued. That notice may explain an adjustment, request information, or provide instructions.

| Code | General description |
| --- | --- |
| 570 | Additional liability pending and/or credit hold |
| 971 | Miscellaneous account action; the action code provides more context |

If both codes appear, do not assume Code 971 always means an audit or a specific refund problem. Check your IRS Online Account for notices and letters. If the IRS requested information, follow the instructions and deadlines in the notice.

## How Long Does IRS Code 570 Last?

There is no single processing timeline for every taxpayer with Code 570. Some account holds may be resolved quickly, while others may require additional review or documents and take longer.

The timing can depend on:

- Why the hold was placed
- Whether the IRS needs to verify income or credits
- Whether additional documents are required
- Whether an adjustment to the return is necessary
- The type of account review involved
- Whether you need to respond to an IRS notice

Avoid relying on claims that every Code 570 hold clears within a specific number of days or weeks. Monitor your transcript for new transactions and your IRS Online Account for notices.

## What Should You Do If You See Code 570?

Do not immediately file an amended return just because Code 570 appears. First, check whether the IRS has asked you to take action.

### Step 1: Open your IRS Account Transcript

Visit [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/), sign in to your IRS Online Account, open **Tax Records**, and select the Account Transcript for the relevant tax year. Find Code 570 in the transactions section.

### Step 2: Review other transaction codes

Do not look at Code 570 in isolation. Review transactions before and after it, including:

- **Code 150:** Return posted and tax assessed
- **Code 806:** Income tax withholding credit
- **Code 766:** Credit to your account
- **Code 768:** Earned Income Tax Credit
- **Code 971:** Miscellaneous account action
- **Code 571:** Reversal of a freeze
- **Code 846:** Refund issued

These codes may provide context about your account activity.

### Step 3: Check for IRS notices

Open your IRS Online Account and look for notices or letters. The IRS may send a notice explaining that your return requires review or that you need to provide information.

Read any notice carefully, follow its instructions, and note the response deadline and documents requested.

### Step 4: Check your refund status

Use the IRS [Where's My Refund?](https://www.irs.gov/wheres-my-refund) tool. You will generally need your SSN or ITIN, filing status, and whole-dollar refund amount. The tool provides the available refund status, which may indicate whether the return was received, approved, or sent.

### Step 5: Contact the IRS when necessary

If your transcript has not changed, you cannot find an explanation for the hold, or the IRS has not provided instructions, consider contacting the IRS using contact information on its official website.

Have your tax return, transcript, and any IRS notices available. Avoid sending documents or personal information through unofficial websites, emails, or social media accounts.

## What Happens After IRS Code 570?

The next transaction depends on why the hold was placed and the result of the IRS review. Possible outcomes include:

### Code 571 appears

If the relevant freeze is released, Code 571 may appear. This generally indicates that the freeze associated with Code 570 was reversed, but it does not automatically mean your refund has been issued.

### Code 846 appears

If the IRS completes processing and releases your refund, Code 846 may appear. The date and amount provide information about the recorded refund issuance. Your bank or postal service may still need time to deliver the payment.

### The IRS adjusts your tax return

The IRS may decide a change is necessary after reviewing your return. It could adjust a credit, withholding amount, or tax liability. Your refund could increase, decrease, or become a balance due. The IRS may send a notice explaining the adjustment and your options if you disagree.

### The IRS requests more information

If supporting documents are needed, the IRS may send a notice explaining what to provide. Respond using the method and deadline specified, and keep copies of everything you submit.

## Does Code 570 Mean You Are Being Audited?

No. **Code 570 does not automatically mean that the IRS is auditing your tax return.** It indicates that additional liability may be pending or that a credit hold is present.

An examination or audit is a separate process. Other transaction codes, such as Code 420, may be associated with an examination. If you see Code 570 without an examination notice or other relevant information, do not assume an audit has started. Review IRS correspondence before drawing conclusions.

## Frequently Asked Questions About IRS Transcript Code 570

### Is IRS Code 570 good news?

Not necessarily. It indicates that additional account action is pending or a credit hold is present, and it may delay your refund. It does not automatically mean your return was rejected or that you owe additional tax.

### Does Code 570 mean my refund has been denied?

No. Code 570 does not mean your refund has been permanently denied. It generally indicates that the IRS has not completed an account action or released a held credit.

### Can Code 570 disappear without Code 571?

Yes. Transcript activity varies depending on account circumstances. Do not assume every hold must follow a particular sequence of codes; review the full transaction history.

### Does Code 570 mean I made a mistake on my tax return?

Not necessarily. The IRS may need time to verify information or resolve an account issue. The code alone does not identify an error or prove that you filed incorrectly.

### Should I amend my return because of Code 570?

No. Do not file an amended return just because Code 570 appears. Review your return and any IRS notices first. Amend only if you identify an error or the IRS instructs you to take a particular action.

### Can Code 570 appear with Code 971?

Yes. Both codes may appear on a transcript. Code 971 represents a miscellaneous account action, and its specific meaning depends on the action code and related information.

### What does Code 570 followed by Code 846 mean?

This may indicate that the IRS placed a hold on your account and later issued your refund. Review the dates and amounts to understand the sequence. Code 846 generally indicates that the refund transaction was issued.

### Can I call the IRS to remove Code 570?

You can contact the IRS to ask about your account, but a representative may not be able to remove the hold immediately. The appropriate action depends on why the hold exists and whether further review or documents are required.

### Will I receive a letter if Code 570 appears?

Not always. Some account actions may not require a notice, while others may result in an IRS letter requesting information or explaining an adjustment. Check your online account and physical mail regularly.

### How can I know why my refund is on hold?

Review transactions around Code 570, check for action codes, and look for IRS notices. If the reason is still unclear, contact the IRS through an official communication channel.

## Final Thoughts

IRS Transcript Code 570 generally indicates that additional liability is pending or a credit hold has been placed on your tax account. It can delay your refund while the IRS reviews or resolves the relevant issue.

However, Code 570 does not automatically mean your return is incorrect, that you are being audited, or that your refund has been denied.

If you see this code, review your Account Transcript, check for IRS notices, and monitor your refund through the IRS website. Look for transactions such as Code 571 or Code 846 to understand whether the hold has been released and your refund has been issued.

The important thing is to understand the full transaction history rather than relying on Code 570 alone.
