# How to Get Your IRS Tax Transcript Online: Step-by-Step Guide

Getting an IRS tax transcript online is one of the quickest ways to review selected information from your federal tax records. You may need one to verify income for a mortgage, review details from a past tax return, or check payments credited to your IRS account.

The IRS lets individual taxpayers view, download, and print available transcripts through an Individual Online Account. The process is generally straightforward, though creating an account and verifying your identity can take extra steps if you are a first-time user.

This guide explains what to prepare, how to sign in, how to choose the right transcript and tax year, and how to download the document. It also covers what to check if you cannot find the transcript you need.

## What Is an IRS Tax Transcript?

An IRS tax transcript is a summary of selected information from your federal tax records. Depending on the transcript type, it may include details about your filed tax return, reported income, payments, and account activity.

Unlike a photocopy of your tax return, a transcript presents selected information in a condensed format. It may show:

- Your filing status
- Adjusted gross income (AGI)
- Taxable income
- Reported income and deductions
- Tax payments and withholding
- Refund information
- Account adjustments and transactions
- Information reported to the IRS by employers and financial institutions

The exact details vary by transcript type. Common options include Tax Return, Tax Account, Record of Account, and Wage and Income Transcripts. These records may be used for mortgage applications, income verification, financial aid, tax preparation, and reviewing past tax activity.

**Important:** IRS tax transcripts are generally available free from the IRS. You do not need to pay a third-party website to access them.

## Can You Get an IRS Tax Transcript Online?

Yes. Individual taxpayers can access eligible transcripts through an [IRS Individual Online Account](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/get-transcript). This is often the most convenient option because you can view, download, or print available records without waiting for mail.

Depending on your account and what information is available, the online account may also let you review payment history, tax balances, prior-year AGI, and certain refund or amended-return information. Online services are generally available around the clock, but maintenance or technical issues can temporarily affect access.

## What to Prepare Before You Start

Gathering the information you may need can make registration and identity verification easier.

### 1. Reliable internet access

You need internet access to visit the IRS website, sign in or register, and download your transcript. A computer can make reviewing and saving a PDF easier, but you can also use a supported browser on another device.

### 2. An active email address

You may need an email address for account registration and identity verification. Make sure you can access it in case the process sends a verification message or security notification.

### 3. Personal identification information

Have your identifying information available. This may include:

- Your legal name
- Social Security number (SSN) or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)
- Date of birth
- Current residential address
- Other information requested during identity verification

The information you provide should match your records.

### 4. A government-issued identification document

Depending on the verification process, you may be asked for a driver's license, passport, or another supported identification document. Requirements vary based on the verification method and your circumstances.

### 5. A smartphone or camera

Identity verification may require a smartphone or computer camera. For example, you may be asked to photograph an identification document or complete another verification step. Follow the instructions shown during registration.

### 6. Previous tax information

You may not need a tax return for every registration, but having recent tax information available can help if you need to verify personal details or troubleshoot an account issue. If you recently moved, remember that your current address may differ from the address on your processed tax return.

## How to Get Your IRS Tax Transcript Online

The IRS Individual Online Account is the online method for accessing individual tax transcripts. Follow these steps.

### Step 1: Visit the official IRS website

Open [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/) or go directly to the IRS [Get Transcript page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/get-transcript). The page explains the available transcript types and ways to request them.

Check that the website address ends in `irs.gov` before entering personal information. Avoid unofficial websites that claim to provide IRS transcript services.

### Step 2: Open the Get Transcript page

On the IRS website, find the option for getting tax records and transcripts. Review the information about accessing records through an Individual Online Account, then choose the sign-in option.

### Step 3: Sign in or create an Individual Online Account

If you already have an account, sign in and follow the instructions. If you are new to the service, choose the registration option and follow the prompts. The IRS uses supported identity-verification services, such as ID.me, to verify users before granting access to tax information.

During registration, you may be asked to:

1. Enter and confirm your email address.
2. Provide identifying information.
3. Complete identity-verification steps.
4. Set up any requested security features.

The exact process may vary depending on the verification method. Your IRS Individual Online Account is separate from your email account; having an email address alone does not provide access to tax records.

### Step 4: Complete identity verification

Because transcripts contain financial information, the IRS needs to verify that you are authorized to access the records. Depending on the verification method, you may be asked to:

- Enter your legal name and date of birth
- Provide your SSN or other requested identifying information
- Upload a photograph of an acceptable identification document
- Use a smartphone or computer camera
- Complete a facial-verification step
- Answer additional security questions

Follow the instructions carefully. If an identification document is not accepted, check that it meets the verification provider's requirements. An unclear photo, expired document, or mismatch in your information may prevent verification. If verification fails, review your details and follow the official instructions rather than repeatedly submitting the same information.

### Step 5: Open your Individual Online Account

After registration and identity verification, sign in to your account. Depending on your account and available records, the dashboard may provide access to tax records, account balances, payment history, prior-year AGI, refund-related information, and available transcripts.

For this task, go to the tax records section.

### Step 6: Go to Tax Records

Locate the **Tax Records** page. Select **Transcripts** to see transcript categories and tax years available for your account.

Review the options before choosing a document. If an organization—such as a mortgage lender—is requesting a transcript, check its requirements so you request the correct type.

### Step 7: Select the right transcript type

Choose the transcript that matches your needs:

- **Tax Return Transcript:** Shows most information from your filed federal income tax return, such as filing status, AGI, and taxable income. It is commonly used for mortgage applications and income verification.
- **Tax Account Transcript:** Shows account activity, which may include payments, penalties, interest, adjustments, and other transactions.
- **Record of Account Transcript:** Combines information from a Tax Return Transcript and a Tax Account Transcript.
- **Wage and Income Transcript:** Shows information from income documents reported to the IRS, such as W-2, 1099, 1098, and 5498 forms.
- **Verification of Non-Filing Letter:** Confirms that the IRS has no record of a processed Form 1040-series return for a tax year as of the request date. It does not establish whether you were required to file.

If you are unsure which document to select, ask the organization requesting it.

### Step 8: Choose the tax year

Select the tax year you need. For example, if a lender requests a 2024 Tax Return Transcript, choose tax year 2024 if it is available.

The tax year is not necessarily the year you request the document. A transcript for tax year 2023 contains information for 2023, even if you are requesting it in a later year. Transcript availability varies by type and year.

### Step 9: View and review the transcript

Follow the on-screen instructions to open the transcript. Depending on its type, it may show taxpayer identification information, filing status, income, AGI, taxable income, payments and withholding, account transactions, or refund-related information.

Confirm that the transcript is for the correct taxpayer and tax year. If you see figures or transaction codes you do not understand, consult the IRS explanations or a qualified tax professional rather than drawing conclusions from a code alone.

### Step 10: Download or print the transcript

Look for the download or print option. If a PDF option is available:

1. Choose the download or print option.
2. Select PDF, if prompted.
3. Save the file somewhere you can find later.
4. Give it a clear name, such as `IRS-Tax-Return-Transcript-2024.pdf`.
5. Open the saved file to confirm it displays correctly.

You can then print the transcript or send it to the organization that requested it. Before emailing it, confirm the recipient and make sure the document is for the correct year.

## If You Cannot Find the Transcript You Need

The online account only displays transcripts and tax years available for your account. If the record is not listed, confirm that you selected the right transcript type and tax year, and check whether that transcript is available yet. The IRS [Get Transcript page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/get-transcript) explains other request options, including mail and phone, for eligible transcripts.

If you cannot complete online identity verification, follow the instructions provided by the IRS or its identity-verification service. Do not share your SSN, tax documents, or account credentials with unofficial sites claiming they can retrieve a transcript for you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it free to get an IRS transcript online?

Yes. The IRS generally provides transcripts free through its official online account and other IRS request methods. Be cautious of third-party sites that charge to obtain a transcript.

### How long does it take to get an IRS transcript online?

If the transcript is available in your Individual Online Account, you can generally view or download it after signing in. Availability depends on the transcript type and tax year.

### Which IRS transcript should I choose?

Choose based on what you need to verify. A Tax Return Transcript contains most information from your original return; a Tax Account Transcript focuses on account activity; a Record of Account combines both; and a Wage and Income Transcript lists information returns reported to the IRS. Confirm the exact requirement with the organization requesting the document.

### Can I get a transcript if I cannot use the online account?

The IRS Get Transcript page lists other request methods, including mail or phone, for eligible documents. Check IRS.gov for the current instructions and options.

## Final Thoughts

An IRS Individual Online Account can make it easier to access available tax transcripts without waiting for mail. Prepare your information, use the official IRS website, complete identity verification, and select the transcript type and year that match your needs.

Before sharing a downloaded transcript, confirm that it is the correct document and send it only to the intended recipient.
