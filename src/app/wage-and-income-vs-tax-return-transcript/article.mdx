# IRS Wage and Income Transcript vs. Tax Return Transcript: What's the Difference?

Understanding the difference between an IRS Wage and Income Transcript and a Tax Return Transcript is important when you need to verify income, review tax information, apply for a loan, or resolve an IRS tax issue. Although both documents are provided by the Internal Revenue Service (IRS), they contain different information and serve different purposes.

A Wage and Income Transcript shows information reported to the IRS by employers, banks, financial institutions, and other organizations. A Tax Return Transcript summarizes most of the information included in your filed federal income tax return.

Knowing which transcript to request can save time and help you avoid delays when submitting documents or reviewing your tax records. This guide explains how both transcripts work, what information they contain, their differences, and how to obtain them.

## What Is an IRS Wage and Income Transcript?

An IRS Wage and Income Transcript is a document containing information returns reported to the IRS by third parties. These organizations include employers, banks, investment companies, retirement plan administrators, and other financial institutions.

For example, employers report employee wages and federal tax withholding through Form W-2. Banks report interest income through Form 1099-INT, while investment companies may report dividends through Form 1099-DIV.

The IRS collects these documents and makes the available information accessible through a Wage and Income Transcript.

This transcript is particularly useful when you have lost an income document or need to verify information reported by an employer or financial institution.

### What Information Does It Include?

Depending on your activities, a Wage and Income Transcript may contain information from several tax forms, including:

- **Form W-2:** Reports wages, salaries, tips, and federal income tax withholding.
- **Form 1099-INT:** Reports interest income from banks and other financial institutions.
- **Form 1099-DIV:** Reports certain investment distributions.
- **Form 1099-NEC:** Reports nonemployee compensation received by contractors.
- **Form 1099-MISC:** Reports miscellaneous income payments.
- **Form 1099-R:** Reports distributions from retirement accounts and pension plans.
- **Form 1099-G:** Reports government payments, including unemployment compensation.
- **Form 1098:** Reports mortgage interest information.
- **Form 5498:** Reports other information about certain retirement accounts.

Not every taxpayer will have all these forms. The information depends on the income received and activities during the tax year.

### Why Do You Need a Wage and Income Transcript?

A Wage and Income Transcript can help when you need to recover tax documents, prepare a tax return, or verify income reported to the IRS.

Common reasons to request one include:

- You lost your W-2 or 1099 form.
- You need to verify income reported by an employer.
- You are preparing an amended tax return.
- You want to check interest or dividend income.
- You need to review retirement distributions.
- You are responding to an IRS notice involving income.

For example, if you worked for two employers during the year but misplaced one W-2, your Wage and Income Transcript may help you recover the wage information reported by both employers.

However, it is not a record of every financial transaction. It only includes information returns received and processed by the IRS.

## What Is an IRS Tax Return Transcript?

An IRS Tax Return Transcript is a document summarizing most of the information from your originally filed federal income tax return.

It generally includes information from Form 1040 and relevant associated forms and schedules. Unlike a Wage and Income Transcript, it focuses on the information you reported when filing your tax return.

For example, if you filed your 2025 tax return and need to verify your adjusted gross income (AGI), you can request a 2025 Tax Return Transcript.

Banks, mortgage lenders, financial aid programs, and other organizations may request this document to verify previously reported tax information.

### What Information Does It Include?

A Tax Return Transcript generally contains:

- Filing status, such as married filing jointly.
- Total income reported on the original return.
- Adjusted gross income (AGI).
- Taxable income.
- Deductions.
- Tax credits.
- Income tax withholding.
- Total tax liability.
- Refund or balance information.
- Other relevant figures from the return.

The exact information depends on the tax return filed.

### Why Do You Need a Tax Return Transcript?

A Tax Return Transcript is useful when an organization needs to verify information from a filed federal income tax return.

You may need one when:

- Applying for a mortgage or refinancing a loan.
- Verifying your year's adjusted gross income.
- Completing financial aid applications.
- Providing tax information to an institution.
- Reviewing your reported income.
- Confirming your filing status and taxable income.

For example, if a mortgage lender requests your tax information for the past two years, it may accept IRS Tax Return Transcripts instead of complete copies of your original returns.

Always confirm the lender's requirements before requesting a transcript.

## IRS Wage and Income Transcript vs. Tax Return Transcript: Key Differences

Both transcripts provide information about your tax records, but their sources, purposes, and contents are different.

### 1. Source of Information

A Wage and Income Transcript contains information submitted to the IRS by employers, banks, financial institutions, and other reporting organizations.

A Tax Return Transcript contains information from your filed federal income tax return.

In simple terms:

- **Wage and Income Transcript:** What third parties reported to the IRS.
- **Tax Return Transcript:** What you reported on your original tax return.

### 2. Type of Information

A Wage and Income Transcript focuses on income documents, including wages, interest, dividends, and retirement distributions.

A Tax Return Transcript focuses on your tax return, including filing status, adjusted gross income, taxable income, deductions, credits, and withholding.

If you want to verify interest reported by your bank, request a Wage and Income Transcript. If you want to confirm your AGI, request a Tax Return Transcript.

### 3. Income Verification

Both transcripts can help verify information, but they are used differently.

A Wage and Income Transcript helps verify income reported by employers and financial institutions. A Tax Return Transcript helps verify information included in your filed federal tax return.

Mortgage lenders and financial institutions may request Tax Return Transcripts, while Wage and Income Transcripts may help investigate incorrect income information.

### 4. Deductions and Tax Credits

A Wage and Income Transcript does not provide a summary of the deductions and credits claimed on your federal tax return.

A Tax Return Transcript generally includes relevant line items from your original return, including deductions and credits.

Therefore, if you want to review deductions or credits claimed on a return, a Tax Return Transcript is generally more appropriate.

### 5. Availability

Both transcripts are associated with tax years, but their availability depends on different processing activities.

Tax Return Transcripts generally become available after the IRS processes your return.

Wage and Income Transcripts depend on when employers, banks, and other organizations submit their information returns and when the IRS processes those documents.

Some current-year income information may be missing when you request a transcript early in the year.

### 6. Amended Tax Returns

A Tax Return Transcript generally reflects the originally filed return and does not show subsequent changes made through an amended return.

A Wage and Income Transcript does not summarize amended return activity either. It shows information from third-party documents.

If you need to review adjustments, payments, or amended return activity, consider requesting a Tax Account Transcript or Record of Account Transcript.

## Which IRS Transcript Should You Request?

The right transcript depends on the information you need.

**Request a Wage and Income Transcript if:**

- You lost your W-2 or 1099.
- You need to verify wages reported by an employer.
- You are preparing a tax return.
- You want to review interest or dividend income.
- You need information about retirement distributions.
- You want to identify income documents reported to the IRS.

**Request a Tax Return Transcript if:**

- You need to verify your adjusted gross income.
- A lender requests tax return information.
- You are applying for a mortgage.
- You need to confirm your filing status.
- You want to review deductions and credits.
- You need proof of information included on your return.

### Should You Request Both Transcripts?

In some situations, requesting both transcripts can help you understand differences between your reported income and originally filed tax return.

For example, suppose your employer reported $60,000 in wages but your original tax return included $55,000.

Your Wage and Income Transcript may show the employer-reported amount, while your Tax Return Transcript may show the amount included in your return.

Comparing the documents can help you identify a reporting error.

## How to Get an IRS Wage and Income Transcript or Tax Return Transcript

The IRS provides several ways to obtain transcripts. The easiest method is generally through your IRS Individual Online Account.

### Method 1: Request a Transcript Online

Follow these steps:

1. Visit [IRS Get Transcript](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/get-transcript).
2. Select the option to access your Individual Online Account.
3. Sign in and complete the required identity verification.
4. Navigate to your tax records or transcripts.
5. Select the tax year you need.
6. Choose Wage and Income Transcript or Tax Return Transcript.
7. View, download, or print the document.

Both transcripts are generally available online at no charge.

### Method 2: Request a Transcript by Mail

You can request transcripts through the IRS Get Transcript by Mail service.

1. Visit the IRS transcript website.
2. Select the mail request option.
3. Provide the required information.
4. Choose the transcript type and tax year.
5. Submit your request.
6. Wait for delivery.

The IRS generally advises allowing five to ten calendar days for transcripts. Availability varies by transcript type, so check the IRS website before submitting your request.

### Method 3: Use Form 4506-T

You can also use Form 4506-T, Request for Transcript of Tax Return.

To request a transcript:

1. Visit the [IRS Form 4506-T page](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-4506-t).
2. Download the form.
3. Enter your identifying information.
4. Specify the tax year.
5. Select the transcript type.
6. Sign and date the form.
7. Submit it using the mailing address or fax number in the instructions.

Read the instructions carefully to avoid delays caused by missing information or incorrect details.

## How Long Does It Take to Receive Each Transcript?

Processing time depends on the transcript type, tax year, and request method.

**Tax Return Transcript:** For filed returns with a refund or no balance due, the IRS generally advises allowing two to three weeks after filing. Paper-filed returns may take six to eight weeks.

**Wage and Income Transcript:** Availability depends on when employers and other organizations submit their information returns. Current-year information generally becomes available in February, although some documents may take longer to appear.

Online access is generally faster when the information is already available.

## Why Do My Two Transcripts Show Different Information?

It is possible for your Wage and Income Transcript and Tax Return Transcript to show different figures.

Common reasons include:

1. **Missing income:** Your original tax return may not include income reported by an employer or financial institution.
2. **Incorrect reporting:** An employer or bank may have submitted incorrect information.
3. **Processing delays:** Some W-2s or 1099s may not have appeared in IRS records when you requested your transcript.
4. **Amended returns:** Your Tax Return Transcript may continue to reflect the originally filed return rather than subsequent changes.
5. **Identification errors:** Incorrect taxpayer identification information may affect how reported documents appear in IRS records.

If you find a discrepancy, compare your transcripts with your tax documents. Contact the organization that issued a W-2 or 1099 and request a correction when necessary.

If your original tax return contains an error, review whether filing Form 1040-X is appropriate.

## IRS Transcript Types You Should Know About

The IRS offers additional transcripts that may help you understand your tax records.

**Tax Account Transcript:** Shows information about your IRS account, including payments, penalties, interest, and certain adjustments.

**Record of Account Transcript:** Combines information from a Tax Return Transcript and Tax Account Transcript. It can help you review your return and subsequent account activity.

**Verification of Non-Filing Letter:** Confirms that the IRS has no record of a processed Form 1040-series return for a year as of the date of the letter. It does not determine whether you were legally required to file.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Is a Wage and Income Transcript the Same as a Tax Return Transcript?

No. A Wage and Income Transcript shows information reported by third parties, while a Tax Return Transcript summarizes information from your originally filed federal income tax return.

### 2. Which Transcript Shows My W-2 Information?

A Wage and Income Transcript generally includes W-2 information, such as wages and federal income tax withholding, when the IRS has received and processed the document.

### 3. Which Transcript Shows My Gross Income?

A Tax Return Transcript generally shows your total income and adjusted gross income from your originally filed federal income tax return.

### 4. Can I Use a Wage and Income Transcript Instead of a W-2?

It can help you recover income information from a missing W-2. However, it may not include all state and local withholding details. Contact your employer if you need a complete replacement W-2.

### 5. Can I Use a Tax Return Transcript for a Mortgage Application?

Yes. Many lenders accept Tax Return Transcripts for income verification. Requirements vary, so confirm the required documents with your lender.

### 6. Are IRS Transcripts Free?

Yes. Eligible Wage and Income and Tax Return Transcripts are available from the IRS at no charge.

### 7. Can I Get Transcripts Online?

Yes. You can generally access both through your IRS Individual Online Account.

### 8. Does a Tax Return Transcript Show Amended Returns?

A standard Tax Return Transcript generally reflects the originally filed return. To review account changes, request a Tax Account Transcript or Record of Account Transcript.

### 9. How Many Years of Wage and Income Transcripts Can I Request?

The IRS generally provides the current tax year and up to nine prior tax years through its Individual Online Account. Availability can vary.

### 10. What Should I Do If My Transcript Contains Incorrect Information?

Compare it with your documents, contact the employer or financial institution responsible for incorrect reporting, and review your tax return for possible errors. Contact the IRS if you need assistance understanding an account discrepancy.

## Final Thoughts

The main difference between an IRS Wage and Income Transcript and a Tax Return Transcript is the source of their information.

A Wage and Income Transcript shows income information reported to the IRS by employers, banks, and other third parties. A Tax Return Transcript summarizes information from your originally filed federal income tax return.

Choose a Wage and Income Transcript when you need to verify W-2s, 1099s, or other reported income documents. Choose a Tax Return Transcript when you need to verify your adjusted gross income, filing status, deductions, credits, or other information from your original return.

If you need to review payments, amended returns, or IRS account adjustments, a Tax Account Transcript or Record of Account Transcript may be more useful.

Understanding these differences helps you request the right document, verify your tax information, and avoid unnecessary delays.
