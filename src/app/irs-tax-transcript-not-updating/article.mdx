# Why Is My IRS Tax Transcript Not Updating? Common Reasons and What to Do

Waiting for your IRS tax transcript to update can be frustrating, especially when you are expecting a refund or trying to understand the status of your tax return. You may have filed weeks ago, but your transcript still shows the same information, displays an old processing date, or says “No Record of Return Filed.”

This does not automatically mean the IRS lost your return or that something is wrong with your filing. Transcript updates depend on when the IRS receives, processes, and posts information to your tax account. Some returns are processed quickly; others require verification, corrections, or manual review.

Understanding how transcripts work can help you decide whether to wait or take action. This guide explains common reasons a transcript may not be updating, general processing timelines, transaction codes to review, and steps to investigate a delay.

## What Does It Mean When Your IRS Tax Transcript Is Not Updating?

An IRS tax transcript summarizes information associated with your tax return or account. It can show filing information, income, payments, credits, adjustments, and other transactions.

When a transcript is not updating, new information may not yet have appeared in the transcript you are viewing. For example:

- The previous tax year's information is still displayed.
- The current-year transcript is unavailable.
- The transcript says “No Record of Return Filed.”
- Your return information appears, but no refund transaction is listed.
- Your account still shows a processing date.
- The transcript has not changed even though you received an IRS notice.
- You are waiting for a transaction such as Code 150 or Code 846 to appear.

These situations can have different explanations. First identify which type of transcript you are checking and what information you expect to see.

**Important:** An IRS transcript is not a real-time tracking screen. It displays information that has become available through IRS transcript and account systems.

## 10 Common Reasons Your IRS Tax Transcript Is Not Updating

### 1. The IRS has not finished processing your tax return

One common reason for an unchanged transcript is that the IRS has not completed processing your return. Processing can include checking the information you submitted, validating figures, calculating tax amounts, and recording the return on your account.

Until the relevant information is processed and posted, your Account Transcript may not show the transactions you expect. The IRS generally processes electronically filed returns within 21 days, although returns requiring corrections or additional review can take longer. Paper-filed returns generally take six or more weeks, according to IRS refund guidance.

If you filed recently, processing may still be underway.

### 2. You are checking your transcript too early

Different transcript types have different availability schedules. For example, current-year Wage and Income Transcript information generally becomes available during the first week of February, after the IRS receives and processes information returns such as W-2s and 1099s.

A Tax Return Transcript may not become available until your return has been processed and the relevant information posted. If you are checking early in the filing season, missing information does not necessarily indicate a problem.

### 3. Your return requires additional review

Some returns need additional review before the IRS can complete processing. This may happen when the IRS needs to verify reported income, withholding, credits, or other information.

Examples include:

- Reported income that needs to be compared with employer or financial institution records
- Information about dependents that requires verification
- Tax credits that need additional checks
- Other return information requiring review

The IRS may take longer to update your account during additional review. Depending on the account activity, you may see codes such as 570 or 971, but these codes do not always explain the reason for a delay.

### 4. Your transcript shows “No Record of Return Filed”

This message can be concerning, but it does not always mean the IRS never received your return. The return may not yet have been processed, or information may not have populated to the transcript you are viewing.

The IRS explains that a current-year Wage and Income Transcript showing “No Record of Return Filed” means the information has not populated to that transcript yet. Also note that a Wage and Income Transcript and a Tax Return Transcript serve different purposes:

- A **Wage and Income Transcript** displays information returns received by the IRS.
- A **Tax Return Transcript** displays information from your processed tax return.

Confirm which transcript you opened before deciding your return is missing.

### 5. You filed a paper tax return

Paper-filed returns generally take longer to process than electronically filed returns. The IRS must receive and handle the document, enter the information into its systems, and complete processing.

The IRS generally advises allowing six or more weeks for a mailed return when checking refund timing. Additional processing or review can extend the wait. Keep your mailing records and delivery confirmation.

### 6. Your return contains errors or missing information

Errors can delay processing and prevent your transcript from updating as expected. Examples include:

- Incorrect Social Security numbers or taxpayer identification numbers
- Missing signatures on a paper return
- Incorrect income figures
- Mathematical errors
- Incorrect bank account information
- Incorrect dependent information
- Missing schedules or supporting information

If the IRS identifies an issue, it may need to correct the return or contact you. It may also change figures during processing. In that case, your Tax Return Transcript may show figures labeled “per return” and corrected figures labeled “per computer.” If you believe the IRS changed something incorrectly, review the transcript and any notice explaining the adjustment.

### 7. Your return includes refundable tax credits

Some tax credits can affect when the IRS releases a refund. Federal law requires the IRS to hold refunds for returns claiming the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) or Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC) until at least mid-February, including the portion of the refund not associated with those credits.

These returns may also require additional processing or verification. This does not automatically mean your credits were rejected. Check the IRS refund tracker and any notices for more information.

### 8. Your account has a processing hold

An account hold can prevent certain transactions from posting or a refund from being released while the IRS reviews an issue.

For example:

- **Code 570** generally indicates additional liability is pending and/or a credit hold is present.
- **Code 810** generally indicates a refund freeze.
- **Code 971** can record a notice or another account action, depending on the description and action code.

These codes do not explain every detail by themselves. Review surrounding transactions and IRS notices.

### 9. You filed an amended tax return

Amended returns follow a separate review and processing path. If you filed Form 1040-X, the original Tax Return Transcript may not show subsequent changes.

A Record of Account Transcript may provide more complete information because it combines return information with account activity. You can also check the amended return status through the IRS Where’s My Amended Return? tool.

### 10. You are checking the wrong tax year or transcript type

The processing year and the tax year are not always the same. For example, if you filed your 2025 return in 2026, check the transcript for tax year 2025—not 2026.

Also make sure the transcript type matches the information you need. A Wage and Income Transcript, Tax Return Transcript, and Account Transcript contain different information and may become available at different times.

## How Long Does It Take for an IRS Tax Transcript to Update?

There is no single schedule for every taxpayer or transcript type. Timing depends on how you filed, what information you need, and whether your return needs additional processing.

| Situation | General IRS timeframe or availability |
| --- | --- |
| E-filed return | Usually processed within 21 days |
| Paper-filed individual return | Refund timing generally takes six or more weeks |
| Current-year Wage and Income Transcript | Usually available during the first week of February |
| Current-year Verification of Non-Filing Letter | Usually available after June 15 |
| Amended tax return | Requires separate review and processing time |

These are general guidelines, not guaranteed transcript-update dates. Returns that need corrections or special handling may take longer. For additional processing details, visit the IRS [Processing Status for Tax Forms](https://www.irs.gov/help/processing-status-for-tax-forms) page.

## What Do IRS Transaction Codes Mean When Your Transcript Is Not Updating?

Transaction codes describe activity recorded on your tax account. Reviewing codes already listed may help you understand what is happening.

| Code | General meaning | What it may indicate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 150 | Tax return filed and tax assessed | Your return has been added to the account |
| 806 | Withholding credit | Federal income tax withholding was recorded |
| 766 | Credit to your account | A credit was added to the account |
| 768 | Earned Income Tax Credit | An EITC transaction was recorded |
| 570 | Additional liability pending and/or credit hold | An account hold or pending issue may exist |
| 571 | Reversal of freeze | The freeze linked to Code 570 was removed |
| 846 | Refund issued | The IRS recorded a refund being issued |
| 971 | Miscellaneous transaction | Could represent a notice or another account action |
| 977 | Amended return filed and posted | An amended return was recorded |

These explanations are general. Dates, amounts, descriptions, and related actions are needed to understand your account. Code 150 does not mean your refund has been issued; Code 846 generally shows that the IRS recorded a refund being issued. Code 971 does not always mean a notice was sent; its meaning depends on the associated action.

## What Should You Do If Your IRS Transcript Is Not Updating?

If your transcript has not changed, take these steps before contacting the IRS.

### Step 1: Make sure you are checking the right tax year

Check that you selected the tax year for the return you filed. If you filed your 2025 return in 2026, look at the transcript for tax year 2025. The processing year and tax year are not always the same.

### Step 2: Choose the right transcript type

The IRS offers different transcript types, each containing different details:

- **Account Transcript:** Account changes and transactions
- **Tax Return Transcript:** Details from your original tax return
- **Record of Account Transcript:** A combination of return and account information
- **Wage and Income Transcript:** Information reported by your employer, bank, or other organizations

### Step 3: Check your refund status

If you are waiting for a refund, use the IRS [Where’s My Refund?](https://www.irs.gov/wheres-my-refund) tool. It usually requires your SSN or ITIN, filing status, and exact whole-dollar refund amount.

The IRS says refund details are usually available:

- 24 hours after e-filing a current-year return
- Three days after e-filing a prior-year return
- Four weeks after mailing a return

The tracker is usually more useful for checking refund status than repeatedly checking your transcript.

### Step 4: Look at your account transactions

Check for codes such as 150, 570, 571, 846, and 971. Compare dates and amounts to understand what happened. If Code 570 is present but Codes 571 and 846 are not, the transcript may not yet show that the freeze was removed or that a refund was issued. This does not explain exactly why processing is delayed, but it can help identify what information is missing.

### Step 5: Check your IRS notices

Sign in to your IRS Online Account and look for notices or letters. The IRS may ask you to:

- Confirm your identity
- Send supporting documents
- Correct certain information
- Respond to a tax adjustment
- Make a payment
- Review an account decision

Read the notice carefully and follow its instructions and deadlines. If the IRS asks for additional information, responding promptly can help avoid further delays.

### Step 6: Keep your filing proof and documents

If you filed electronically, keep your e-file confirmation. Acceptance means the IRS received your return; it does not mean the return has been fully processed or your refund approved.

If you mailed your return, keep a copy and any proof of mailing or delivery. These records can help if you need to contact the IRS.

### Step 7: Contact the IRS when needed

If the usual processing time has passed, your transcript has not changed, or the refund tool tells you to contact the IRS, consider calling. Official contact details are available on the IRS [Telephone Assistance](https://www.irs.gov/help/telephone-assistance) page.

Prepare your SSN or ITIN, correct tax year, filed return, Account Transcript, e-file confirmation or mailing details, and any IRS notices. Explain when you filed and what currently appears on your transcript.

## Should You File Your Tax Return Again If Your Transcript Is Not Updating?

Usually, no. Do not file another return just because your transcript has not updated.

If you filed electronically and received confirmation that the IRS accepted your return, filing again can create problems and slow things down. If you mailed your return, give the IRS time to process it before taking further action.

Do not file Form 1040-X just to force a transcript update. An amended return is for correcting a previously filed return. If you are unsure whether your return was received, check your e-file confirmation, mailing records, and IRS refund status first.

## IRS Transcript Not Updating After Code 570: What Does It Mean?

If your transcript shows Code 570 and nothing changes, the IRS may still be working on the account action linked to the hold. Code 570 generally means additional liability is pending and/or a credit hold has been placed on the account.

Possible next steps may include checking income or withholding information, verifying tax credits, making an account adjustment, sending a notice requesting information, or removing the freeze through Code 571. Code 570 alone does not explain the exact reason for the hold or when it will be removed.

Check your transcript for additional entries and review IRS notices. If the IRS requests documents, follow the instructions for submitting them.

## IRS Transcript Not Updating After Code 971: What Does It Mean?

Code 971 may mean the IRS sent a notice or recorded another account action. If it appears and nothing else changes, that does not necessarily mean processing has stopped.

Review the description and any associated action code. Then check your IRS Online Account or physical mail for a matching notice. If the entry relates to an amended return or another account action, the next update may depend on the department handling it.

## Frequently Asked Questions About IRS Transcripts Not Updating

### Why is my IRS tax transcript still showing last year's information?

You may be viewing a tax year or transcript that has not received current-year details yet. Confirm the tax year and transcript type. Current-year details become available according to the IRS transcript release schedule.

### What does “No Record of Return Filed” mean?

It means the transcript you are viewing does not currently show a processed return. It does not always mean the IRS never received your return. Check your filing confirmation and the correct refund-status tool.

### How often does the IRS update tax transcripts?

There is no single update schedule for everyone. Transcript details change when account transactions and information become available through IRS systems. Some accounts need additional processing time.

### Can my IRS transcript update without showing Code 846?

Yes. A transcript can show transactions and account changes without a refund being issued. Code 846 generally shows that the IRS recorded a refund being issued.

### Does an unchanged transcript mean my tax return was rejected?

No. An unchanged transcript does not automatically mean your return was rejected. Check your e-file acceptance confirmation or use the IRS refund tracker to see whether the IRS received your return.

### Why is my Wage and Income Transcript not showing my W-2?

Current-year Wage and Income Transcript information generally becomes available during the first week of February. The IRS must receive and process information returns before displaying them. If information is still missing after the expected availability period, contact your employer or the organization that issued the document.

### Why is my Tax Return Transcript not available?

Your return may not have finished processing or been posted to the IRS system. You may also be requesting a tax year or transcript type that is not yet available through the service.

### Does Code 150 mean my transcript is fully updated?

No. Code 150 generally means the return was posted and tax assessed. Additional transactions, adjustments, credits, or refund information may appear later.

### Should I call the IRS if my transcript has not updated for three weeks?

If you e-filed, the IRS generally processes returns within 21 days, but some take longer. First check Where’s My Refund? If the tool tells you to contact the IRS or the normal processing period has passed without an update, consider calling.

### Can an IRS notice explain why my transcript is not updating?

Yes. A notice may explain that additional information, verification, or an account adjustment is needed. Check your IRS Online Account and physical mail for letters.

### Does filing an amended return stop my transcript from updating?

Not necessarily. Amended returns follow a separate processing procedure, and the original Tax Return Transcript may not show later changes. A Record of Account Transcript may provide more complete details.

### Can I get my transcript by mail if it is not available online?

Yes. The IRS allows taxpayers to request transcripts by mail and through its automated phone service. You can also use Form 4506-T when necessary. Availability depends on transcript type and tax year.

## Final Thoughts

An IRS tax transcript that is not updating does not automatically mean your return was lost, rejected, or denied. The delay could be caused by processing time, an unavailable transcript type, additional verification, filing mistakes, an amended return, or an account hold.

First, make sure you selected the correct tax year and transcript type. Then review transaction codes, check refund status through Where’s My Refund?, and look for IRS letters that may require action.

If your return is still within the normal processing period, you may simply need to wait. If the expected processing time has passed or the IRS requests additional information, follow official instructions and contact the IRS when necessary.

Use your transcript as a record of account activity rather than as a real-time refund tracker. Understanding the transactions can help you make informed decisions while waiting for your tax information to update.
